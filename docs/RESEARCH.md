# Why Retroloop exists

Market research done before writing code (October 2026). The goal was to find a
reason for a real team to try one more retrospective tool, not to build a clone.

## The market is crowded, and free is not a differentiator

Free retrospective boards already exist:

| Tool | Free offer (third-party listings, verify before quoting) |
|---|---|
| GoRetro | Free forever, unlimited boards, teams and members; English only |
| Parabol | Free "Starter" plan for up to 2 teams; paid Team plan per active user |
| ScatterSpoke | Free for up to 15 team members |
| Retrospected | Open source |
| EasyRetro | Free plan limited to a few public boards per month |

Competing on price would not give anyone a reason to switch.

## The real gap: action items don't get done

Teams discuss the same problems sprint after sprint because the decisions from
the last retro are lost. The numbers below come from vendors and community polls,
not independent research, so they are indicative only:

- Easy Agile: across hundreds of teams, **less than half** of retrospective action
  items ever got completed.
- A PMI community poll: nearly two-thirds of respondents implemented **fewer than
  25%** of the ideas from their retros.
- TeamRetro data: about **1 in 10** action items had a due date, and items with both
  an owner and a due date were completed about **90%** of the time.

Common advice — limit items to 1–3, review last retro's items first, give every
item an owner and a date — is easy to say and rarely enforced by the tools.

## Other complaints in reviews

- Exporting action items to Jira is missing or awkward (Parabol reviews).
- Cards cannot be exported to a report (older EasyRetro reviews).
- Participants get disconnected mid-session (Parabol reviews).
- Most tools are English-only; no retrospective tool with a Ukrainian interface
  was found.

## Positioning

**Retrospectives where the action items actually get done.**

| Problem | What Retroloop does |
|---|---|
| Items without owner or date | An action item cannot be saved without an owner and a due date; at most 3 per retro |
| Last retro is forgotten | Every retro opens with "what did we do from last time?"; unfinished items carry over |
| Nobody remembers the deadline | The owner gets a reminder before the due date |
| No feedback loop | A per-team "decisions done" rate, sprint over sprint |
| Items stuck in the tool | Export to Jira, GitHub Issues and Markdown |
| Dropped connections | Reconnect and resync without losing or duplicating cards |
| English only | Interface in English, Ukrainian, Portuguese and Russian |

The "decisions done" rate is also the product's main success metric: the target is
to beat the under-50% industry figure for teams using Retroloop, and to report the
real number in the README once there are users.

## Still to validate

Everything above comes from desk research. Before launch it needs a check with
3–5 team leads or Scrum Masters:

1. What do you run retrospectives in, and what annoys you about it?
2. How many items from your last retro actually got done?
3. Would you try a tool that reminds owners and shows the completion rate?
